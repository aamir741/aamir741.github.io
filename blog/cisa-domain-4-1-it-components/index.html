CISA Domain 4.1: IT Components

CISA Domain 4.1: the Domain 4 overview, networking models and devices, hardware and proxies, removable media and wireless, hardware maintenance and tracing a service through its components.

Muhammad Aamir · 28 November 2026

A payment application's servers show full uptime, but customers cannot pay because name resolution and the identity provider have failed. Is the service available? On the CISA exam, no. This lesson covers CISA Domain 4, Section 4.1: IT components, with a quick overview of the whole domain first.

Domain 4 overview

Domain 4, Information Systems Operations and Business Resilience, represents 26 percent of the CISA exam. Reliable operations make services dependable in normal conditions; business resilience prepares the enterprise to continue or recover when conditions change. Sections 4.1 to 4.11 cover operations, and sections 4.12 to 4.16 cover resilience: business impact analysis, system and operational resilience, backup, business continuity and disaster recovery.

The mindset for the whole domain: a successful backup job is not proof of a successful restore, and a server responding to a ping is not proof that users can complete their work.

Networking

IT components are the physical and logical building blocks that deliver services. Start with the service architecture and data flows, not an isolated equipment list. Apply access, environmental, location and recovery controls to the whole information processing facility (IPF), including its support dependencies.

TCP provides an ordered, reliable byte stream; UDP supplies datagrams without equivalent delivery guarantees; IP routes packets but does not itself guarantee delivery. A LAN typically serves a limited location, a WAN connects dispersed locations, and VLANs create logical segmentation, although traffic between them still needs suitable routing and access controls. A star's central device or a ring's unprotected link can be a failure dependency.

Term Control implication
Intranet Internal location alone does not establish safe access
Extranet Check identity, permitted information and boundary controls
ISP Check the contract, actual availability and shared dependencies
MAC address Can often be changed or spoofed, so it is not strong user authentication

A network operations center (NOC) monitors service health; a security operations center (SOC) focuses on security monitoring and response, and the two must coordinate. IPv4 uses 32-bit addresses and IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses; IPv6 does not automatically make a network secure. NAT maps addresses and PAT can multiplex connections using ports, but NAT is not a substitute for firewall policy, authentication or encryption. Convergence on shared infrastructure can reduce cost while concentrating availability and capacity risk, and VoIP needs assessment of signaling, authentication, latency, packet loss and power dependencies.

Hardware and devices

The CPU executes instructions, RAM holds active working data and normally loses it without power, persistent storage retains data, and firmware provides low-level device functions. Multicore and multiprocessor designs do not guarantee a workload runs faster. Virtualization puts many virtual machines on shared hosts, so a failure or overcommitment at that layer can affect many services.

Switches connect local devices, routers direct traffic between networks, firewalls enforce traffic rules, intrusion detection systems report suspicious activity while intrusion prevention systems can block it, and load balancers distribute requests. A forward proxy acts on behalf of clients; a reverse proxy sits in front of servers and can support access control, load distribution or TLS termination. Proxying changes the traffic path and visibility, so inspect certificate handling, logging and the protection of decrypted data.

Removable media and wireless

Removable storage creates risks of data disclosure, malware introduction, loss and uncontrolled transfer. Define permitted use, restrict devices and ports, encrypt approved media and monitor transfers. Encryption protects confidentiality if keys are protected; it does not make an infected file safe. Not every USB device is storage.

For wireless LANs (Wi-Fi commonly uses IEEE 802.11), review access points, authentication, encryption, segmentation and rogue-device detection, and separate guest and business access. A backup cellular link may still share power, building access or provider infrastructure with the primary service. GSM is a legacy term; assess the actual protections rather than assuming the technology name guarantees security.

Hardware maintenance

Preventive maintenance reduces avoidable failures through planned servicing and replacement; corrective maintenance repairs a fault. Set authorized maintenance windows, retain service records and protect data and access during supplier work. Working on a live UPS or shared power path can immediately interrupt service, so verify isolation and bypass procedures. Review hardware errors, utilization trends, fault alerts, recurring incidents, maintenance history and end-of-support status. Maintenance records show activity; declining failures and successful controlled tests provide evidence about results.

Auditor perspective: trace the service

Start with a business transaction, then map the application, compute, network, storage and identity components needed to complete it. Verify the diagram with configuration and service records, and distinguish a component fault from the resulting service interruption. Redundant servers improve availability only to the extent that shared dependencies and capacity support continued processing. A hardware inventory without these relationships gives limited support for impact analysis and recovery planning.

Practice questions

1. An application's servers show full uptime, yet users cannot complete transactions. Which approach would BEST help the IS auditor find the cause? Answer: trace a representative transaction through each dependency it needs.

2. A wireless network allows only approved MAC addresses, and management says unauthorized devices are therefore prevented from connecting. What is the auditor's BEST response? Answer: explain that addresses can often be spoofed, so stronger authentication is needed.

3. Maintenance records show every scheduled service on the data center's power equipment was completed on time. What would provide the BEST evidence that the maintenance is effective? Answer: declining failure rates and successful controlled tests of the equipment.
