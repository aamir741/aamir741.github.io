CISA Domain 3.9: Domain 3 Recap, Memory Aids and Exam Traps

CISA Domain 3.9 recap: memory aids for acquisition, development and implementation, the common exam traps, an integrated case, decision owners and control purpose.

Muhammad Aamir · 28 November 2026

A data migration finishes. The record counts match, all tests passed, and the developers have signed off. Is the system ready for go-live? On the exam, the tempting answer is usually wrong. This lesson covers CISA Domain 3, Section 3.9, The Last Mile: a recap of the whole domain, the memory aids and the exam traps.

Memory aids

Use these after studying the concepts. They support recall; the facts, objectives and qualifiers in a question determine the best answer.

Recall prompt Meaning
WHY, WHAT, HOW, PROVE, MOVE, REVIEW Justify the investment; define requirements; design, build or acquire; test and accept; implement; review outcomes. Work may overlap or iterate
Owner accepts; auditor evaluates Keep business acceptance and risk decisions with the authorized owners
Trace both ways Every important requirement needs evidence; every delivered change needs an approved basis
Count + value + identity + meaning Migration checks need populations, totals, identifiers and correctly transformed detail
ACID All-or-none updates; defined rules; protected concurrency; persistent committed results
Range has two rails; limit has one boundary Distinguish an allowed interval from a single threshold
Accepted = deployed Check artifacts, dependencies and parameters, not only the release filename
Benefits outlive the project Assign ownership and measurement after administrative closure

Calculation check: ROI needs a stated period; payback concerns recovery timing; NPV discounts cash flows. Total float uses early and late dates. A favorable EVM ratio does not prove accepted quality or realized benefit.

Exam traps

Tempting wrong answer Exam answer
All tests passed, so the system is ready Check requirements coverage, critical scenarios, defects and operational readiness
Record counts prove migrated data are accurate Counts and totals support reconciliation but can conceal substitutions and offsetting errors; validate populations, identities, significant values and transformations
A hash total is a cryptographic digest An arithmetic batch total and a cryptographic hash are different
Choose direct cutover for a critical system to save cost Direct cutover concentrates transition risk but can have a tested fallback; parallel operation adds cost, workload and reconciliation risks
Write UAT test cases after system testing ends Acceptance criteria are defined during requirements definition so tests trace to requirements
Agile removes documentation and controls Retain appropriate current evidence throughout delivery
Developer sign-off establishes UAT Business users perform and sign off UAT
A backup is a rollback plan Include restoration, dependencies, new transactions, timing and reconciliation
Persistent user workarounds are a coding defect A mismatch between specifications and business needs can indicate deficient requirements; investigate first
QA, QAT and UAT mean the same thing Distinguish quality processes, technical acceptance and business acceptance
Continue after major cost or scope changes Re-evaluate the business case first
The PIR is only a budget check The PIR checks whether objectives and benefits were achieved, after adequate operation, led by someone independent
Add developers to a late project This usually delays it further; re-baseline or reduce scope
Developers move their own code into production This breaks segregation of duties; an independent function migrates approved changes

Decision owners

Role Responsibility Boundary
Sponsor Owns business justification and business support Delivery outputs are not automatically realized benefits
Project manager Coordinates scope, schedule, cost, dependencies and issues Escalates decisions beyond delegated authority
Business owner/users Define needs and accept business suitability Developer sign-off is not UAT
Operations/technical owners Accept technical and support readiness within their duties Business and residual-risk acceptance remain authorized decisions

Threats and control purpose

The classification of a control depends on how it is configured and used.

Risk Preventive Detective Corrective
Incorrect business processing Approved requirements and embedded authorization and validation Tests, reconciliations and controlled acceptance Fix, retest and deploy the accepted version
Conversion loss or corruption Approved mappings, controlled conversion and rehearsals Population, identity, value and relationship reconciliation Correct exceptions or execute a tested fallback

An integrated case

A payroll replacement has been migrated and is about to go live. The developers say it meets requirements, the record counts match, go-live is a direct cutover with a backup, and the project manager wants the project team to do the review before closing. That is four separate issues: developer sign-off is not UAT, counts do not prove accuracy, direct cutover needs a tested fallback, and the postimplementation review belongs after operation and should be independent. Name each issue, then answer the qualifier in the question.

Practice questions

1. After a data migration, the record counts and control totals match those of the source system. Which action BEST supports a conclusion that the migrated data are accurate? Answer: validate identities, significant values and transformations on a sample. Counts can hide substitutions and offsetting errors.

2. A critical payroll system is scheduled for direct cutover, and management cites the cost saving. What should the IS auditor consider MOST important? Answer: that a tested fallback exists, covering restoration, timing and reconciliation.

3. The developers have signed off that the new system meets its requirements, and the project manager treats this as user acceptance. What is the BEST response? Answer: require business users to perform and sign off acceptance testing.
