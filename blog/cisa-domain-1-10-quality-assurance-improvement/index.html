CISA Domain 1.10: Quality Assurance and Improvement of the Audit Process

CISA Domain 1.10: audit committee oversight, quality assurance, external assessment, team training, monitoring and proving improvement worked.

Muhammad Aamir · 15 November 2026

An audit report can look polished and still miss its own objective. This lesson covers CISA Domain 1, Section 1.10: how an audit function checks the quality of its own work, and how it proves that an improvement actually worked.

Audit quality is the degree to which the work follows applicable requirements and supports useful, defensible conclusions. Quality assurance evaluates whether the function applies its methodology effectively. Quality improvement addresses recurring weaknesses and checks whether the changes help.

1.10.1 Audit committee oversight

The audit committee oversees the audit function on behalf of the governing body. It considers the function's mandate, independence, risk coverage, resources, significant results and quality arrangements. It challenges unresolved issues and receives escalation when management's actions or accepted risk warrant attention.

Oversight supports audit independence, but audit leadership remains responsible for managing and reviewing the work.

1.10.2 Audit quality assurance

An audit can miss its objective through poor planning, weak evidence or inadequate review, even when its report looks professional. The governing body oversees the function; audit leadership owns the methods, supervision, review and quality of its work.

Quality activity What good evidence looks like
Engagement supervision Approved objectives and program; timely review of test work, exceptions and conclusions
Working paper review A reviewer can trace the report to sufficient relevant evidence
Independence monitoring Conflicts declared, assessed, addressed and reconsidered when circumstances change
Training and development Skills assessed against the audit plan; continuing education tracked
Methodology monitoring Recurring review errors lead to updated procedures, templates, training or tools
Function performance Balanced measures of coverage, timeliness, quality, usefulness and follow up

Internal review, external assessment and peer review

  • Internal quality activities: supervision, working paper review, self assessment and performance monitoring.
  • An external quality assessment gives an independent perspective on the function's conformance and effectiveness.
  • A peer review uses qualified people outside the work under review. Its scope and independence determine what assurance it provides.

Internal monitoring is not a substitute for a required independent external assessment. Take the requirements and intervals from the framework actually adopted, and do not borrow an interval from another framework without establishing that it applies.

Improvement in practice

Several audits contain unvalidated spreadsheet populations. Audit leadership makes reconciliation a required planning step, trains staff and checks later files for compliance. Merely recording the recurring weakness would not demonstrate improvement. Track actions, responsibilities and completion, then check whether the change corrected the problem.

1.10.3 Audit team training and development

  • Maintain a documented method, a risk-appropriate review process and a funded development plan for each role.
  • Track training, continuing professional education and applicable certifications, with responsibility for deadlines.
  • Reassess skills when technologies and the audit plan change.
  • Monitor independence declarations throughout the year, not only on joining.

Completing a course, issuing reports faster or closing review notes does not by itself demonstrate better assurance.

1.10.4 Monitoring

Compare delivery with the charter, the risk-based plan and quality objectives. Useful measures include significant risk coverage, timeliness, evidence quality, stakeholder usefulness, overdue actions and recurring review findings. No single count proves effectiveness.

A function that meets every deadline but repeatedly misses cloud services from its audit universe should update the universe, reconcile it to procurement and asset information, reassess coverage and check that future plans include the exposure. Faster reporting would not address the cause.

Read quality indicators together: fewer findings can mean better controls, weak coverage or reluctance to challenge.

Did the improvement work?

A supervisory review finds query outputs with no record of source filters or completeness checks. Correct the affected files, determine whether the cause is methodology, competence or review, update the procedure, train staff, then examine later files for reproducible results. Training attendance shows participation; later files show whether the weakness was fixed.

Practice questions

1. An audit function's self-assessment shows full conformance with its methodology. Its adopted framework requires a periodic external assessment. What is the MOST appropriate conclusion?

Answer: an independent external assessment is still required. Internal monitoring is not a substitute, and a peer review must use people from outside the work under review.

2. During a payroll audit, the data extract omitted a recently acquired subsidiary and the report is due tomorrow. What should the IS auditor do FIRST?

Answer: assess the effect of the omission on scope and population, then obtain and validate the missing data and communicate the limitation. Do not issue an unqualified conclusion just to meet the deadline.
