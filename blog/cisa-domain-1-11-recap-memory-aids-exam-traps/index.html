CISA Domain 1.11: Domain 1 Recap, Memory Aids and Exam Traps

CISA Domain 1.11 recap: five deciding questions, essential distinctions, memory aids like CPP and ACVS, common exam traps and decision owners.

Muhammad Aamir · 16 November 2026

Management disagrees with your finding. Do you remove it? On the exam, the tempting answer is usually wrong. This lesson covers CISA Domain 1, Section 1.11, The Last Mile: a recap of the whole domain, the memory aids and the exam traps.

1.11.1 Five deciding questions

Domain 1 questions often turn on the auditor's role, the stage of work, the purpose of a test or the quality of evidence. Explain your choice rather than picking an answer because it contains a familiar phrase.

Ask yourself What to distinguish
Who owns this decision? Board, audit leadership or management
Where are we in the process? Establishing the function, planning, or one engagement
What is being tested? A control's operation or the correctness of the underlying data
What supports the conclusion? Relevant, reliable evidence for the right population and period
What is already established? A concern, a supported finding, or an urgent condition

Foundation check: define risk before selecting coverage, and distinguish the risk assessment process from a score.

1.11.2 Essential distinctions

Pair Recall
Charter and scope Authority for the function versus boundaries of one engagement
Plan and program Coverage over time versus detailed test procedures
Design and operation Could the control work? Did it actually work?
Detection and residual risk Auditor may miss the problem versus business exposure remaining after controls
Compliance and substantive Test the control versus test the transaction or data
Attribute and variable Characteristic or rate versus quantity or value
Sufficient and appropriate Enough evidence versus relevant and reliable evidence
Recommendation and risk acceptance Audit advises; an authorized management role decides

1.11.3 Memory aids

  • CPP: Charter gives authority; Plan gives coverage; Program gives procedures.
  • ACVS: Attribute sampling with Compliance testing; Variable sampling with Substantive testing.
  • ICeD: Inherent × Control × Detection, the conceptual audit risk model.
  • SRRU: Sufficient, Reliable, Relevant, Useful.
  • Prevent, detect, correct: stop the event; identify it; recover from its effects. Backup and restore is corrective, not preventive.
  • Reality → records: to find unauthorized or undocumented changes, start from the actual system changes and trace them to approvals.
  • CCCER: Condition, Criteria, Cause, Effect, Recommendation.
  • Observe without owning and Owners accept, auditors advise: management owns controls, remediation and risk acceptance.
  • Real data, own program: parallel simulation. Dummy entity, live processing: ITF.

1.11.4 Exam traps

Tempting wrong answer Exam answer
Treating engagement scope or a management request as audit's authority The board or audit committee approved charter is the source of authority
Letting the auditee approve the audit program Audit management approves it; the auditee may clarify facts
Treating residual risk as detection risk Residual is business exposure; detection concerns audit procedures
Sampling from change documentation to find undocumented changes Sample actual system changes and trace them to documentation
Accepting a manager's oral statement as sufficient Obtain appropriate corroboration
Increasing the sample size to fix a flawed procedure A wrong procedure is nonsampling risk
Assuming a full-population CAAT test cannot be wrong Validate the extract's completeness and the script's logic
Removing a finding because management disagrees Retain it and include management's response
Overruling management's acceptance of a risk Escalate to the audit committee or board

1.11.5 Decision owners

The audit committee or board oversees the audit mandate and independence but does not test. The chief audit executive directs the audit function but does not own management controls. The engagement auditor plans, tests and concludes but does not accept the auditee's residual risk. The process owner operates controls and implements corrective action but cannot determine the audit opinion.

Practice questions

Question 1. An attribute sample of 60 user access reviews shows a deviation rate well below the tolerable rate. The auditor then learns the sample was drawn from reviews saved in a shared folder rather than from the system list of required reviews. What is the GREATEST concern?

A) Reviews that were missed could not enter the sample B) The tolerable rate was set without management input C) The sample size is too small for the confidence level D) The deviation rate may understate the sampling risk

Answer: A. Sampling completed reviews excludes reviews never done, so the test cannot find the deviation it targets. A larger sample would not fix that.

Question 2. During follow-up, an IS auditor learns that management has accepted the risk of an unremediated high-risk finding. The auditor believes the residual risk exceeds the organization's risk appetite. Each of the following is an appropriate action EXCEPT:

A) Discuss the basis for acceptance with senior management B) Escalate the matter to the audit committee for resolution C) Reopen the finding and require remediation by management D) Document management's acceptance in the follow-up file

Answer: C. Requiring remediation would make audit the decision maker over risk. The other three are appropriate.
