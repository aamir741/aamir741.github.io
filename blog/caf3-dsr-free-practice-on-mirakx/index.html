Free CAF-3 DSR Exam Preparation: 1,000+ MCQs, Mock Exams, AI Marking and a Study Guide

Everything CAF-3 Data, Systems and Risks students can use free on mirakx.com: 1,020 MCQs, ICAP-style mocks, AI marking of handwritten answers and a readiness score.

Muhammad Aamir · 27 October 2026

If you are preparing for ICAP CAF-3 Data, Systems and Risks (DSR), you can now prepare end to end on mirakx.com, free. I teach this subject, and these are the tools I wanted my own students to have: something to read, something to practise with every day, and a way to sit a real-looking paper before the exam.

Here is what is available and how to use it.

1. The free CAF-3 DSR study guide

A 457-page study guide covering all 17 chapters of the CAF-3 syllabus in plain language, written for students without an IT background. Every chapter has local examples, exam tips, sticky-note revision points, practice MCQs and scenario questions with model answers.

👉 Download the free study guide (PDF)

2. Chapter-wise MCQ practice: 1,020 questions

The CAF-3 DSR Exam Prep section has a page for every chapter, with 60 MCQs per chapter and an explanation for every answer.

  • You choose how many questions: 10, 20, 30, all 60, or any number you type.
  • Learn mode: see the answer and explanation straight after each question. Best when you have just finished reading a chapter.
  • Test mode: timed at 1.5 minutes per question, like the exam, with your score and a full review at the end.
  • Questions you haven't seen come first, so every session is fresh.
  • Retry your wrong answers with one click.
  • Options are shuffled every time, so you learn the concept, not the position of the answer.

Every question has been checked against the ICAP CAF-3 syllabus and the ICAP Study Text, so you are practising what is actually examinable.

3. The mock exam builder: a different paper every time

The mock exam builder follows the ICAP paper pattern: 7 questions, 100 marks.

Part What you get How
Question 1 20 MCQs, 1 mark each Online, timed (30 minutes), marked instantly
Questions 2 to 7 Descriptive and scenario questions (80 marks) Downloadable PDF paper, plus a separate model-answer PDF

You pick one chapter or several, so you can sit a mock after finishing a few chapters, not just at the end. Each paper is generated for you from a bank of 136 descriptive questions and the full MCQ bank, and questions you have already had come last. In testing, ten full papers in a row shared not a single question.

4. AI marking of your handwritten answers

Writing answers is only half the work; you also need to know how they would be marked. After Question 1, write Questions 2 to 7 on paper, by hand, just as you will in the exam. Then:

  1. Take a clear photo of each answer page, in order (or scan them into one PDF).
  2. Upload them on the mock page and tap Mark my answers.
  3. In a minute or two you get marks and feedback for every part, an overall comment on what to improve, and your total out of 100 (Question 1 plus Questions 2 to 7).

The AI marks like a fair examiner, against the model answer and marking guidance: it credits valid points written in your own words, and tells you what was missing. You can have 2 papers marked per day. AI marking is a strong estimate rather than an official result, so check any mark you disagree with against the model-answer PDF. If you prefer, you can still mark yourself.

Write only your paper code on the sheets, not your name. AI marking requires signing in with Google.

5. Your exam readiness score

The My progress page turns everything you do into one number: your exam readiness score out of 100.

  • It combines your recent MCQ accuracy and your marked written answers for every chapter.
  • It is weighted by the ICAP syllabus: Data 35–45%, Systems 30–40%, Risks 20–30%.
  • A chapter only counts fully once you have answered about 20 questions in it, so a few lucky answers can't make you look ready.
  • It shows the chapters to work on next, with links straight to their practice pages, and your full history of attempts.

Sign in with Google and your results follow you across your phone and laptop.

How I suggest you use it

  1. Read the chapter in the study guide.
  2. Practise in Learn mode: 20 questions, reading every explanation, even for answers you got right.
  3. Switch to Test mode a few days later, and retry your wrong answers until they are right.
  4. Sit a chapter mock after every three or four chapters, and get Questions 2 to 7 marked.
  5. Check your readiness score every week and work on the chapters it flags.
  6. In the last month, sit a full-syllabus mock every week, in exam conditions: 3 hours 15 minutes, no notes.

Keep the syllabus weighting in mind as you plan: Data 35–45%, Systems 30–40%, Risks 20–30%.

Good to know

  • Free. Sign in once with Google (recommended) or a short form (name, email, mobile, city and institute), so I can see how students are doing and improve the questions. Details are in the privacy notice.
  • Works on mobile and laptop. With Google sign-in your progress is saved across devices; otherwise it stays on the device you use.
  • Uploaded answer sheets are marked by Google's Gemini AI and kept privately so marks can be checked.
