CISA Domain 2.12: Domain 2 Recap, Memory Aids and Exam Traps

CISA Domain 2.12 recap: memory aids for governance, risk, sourcing and quality, the common exam traps, an integrated case, decision owners and control purpose.

Muhammad Aamir · 27 November 2026

Your cloud supplier's SOC 2 Type II report is clean, but its period ended four months before your service went live. Can you rely on it? On the exam, the tempting answer is usually wrong. This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.12, The Last Mile: a recap of the whole domain, the memory aids and the exam traps.

Memory aids

Use these after studying the chapter. Read the facts in each scenario before applying a familiar phrase: the correct role, artifact, control or measure depends on the decision being asked about.

Memory aid Meaning and limit
Directors direct; managers deliver Governance evaluates, directs and monitors. Management plans and performs the work. Both need evidence of results.
EDM; APO BAI DSS MEA EDM is the COBIT governance domain. The four management domains organize planning, implementation, service and assessment.
Strategy looks ahead; steering sets priorities The strategy committee advises the board. The steering committee coordinates business and IT priorities within delegated authority.
Owner decides; custodian implements Business owners decide classification and access. Custodians implement handling and protection.
Policy gives direction; procedure gives steps Standards set mandatory rules, baselines set minimum levels, guidelines advise.
Identify, assess, respond, monitor Establish context and criteria first, communicate throughout, repeat as conditions change.
TAMA: Transfer, Avoid, Mitigate, Accept Transfer does not erase the enterprise's retained obligations.
Appetite chooses; tolerance bounds; capacity constrains Appetite is the desired amount and type; tolerance gives specific limits; capacity is the ability to bear exposure.
Single loss, annual frequency, annual loss SLE = AV × EF. ALE = SLE × ARO. Keep periods and units consistent.
The work moves; accountability stays A supplier can perform a service. The enterprise still needs an owner, evidence, monitoring and an exit route.
Performance, goal, risk, control KPI measures performance; KGI an outcome; KRI exposure; KCI a control.
QA checks the approach; QC checks the output Classify by the activity's purpose, not the department name.
DMAIC and PDCA Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control; Plan, Do, Check, Act. A completed action must still produce a measured result.

Two scorecards: the enterprise balanced scorecard uses Financial, Customer, Internal process, and Learning and growth. A common IT adaptation uses Corporate contribution, User orientation, Operational excellence and Future orientation.

CMMI: 0 Incomplete, 1 Initial, 2 Managed, 3 Defined, 4 Quantitatively Managed, 5 Optimizing. A higher maturity level is not certification that every application is secure.

Exam traps

Tempting wrong answer Exam answer
Having the board select or configure security controls The board directs and oversees; management selects and implements controls
Ranking IT investments by lowest cost Rank by alignment with business objectives and value over the life cycle
Treating an approved IT strategy as proof of alignment Funded IT initiatives trace to stated business objectives
Treating a quantitative risk estimate as objective fact The result is only as good as its assumptions; review source data and uncertainty
Rejecting a legally required control because it costs more than the ALE it removes Legal and regulatory requirements apply regardless
Treating unaddressed risk as accepted risk An authorized risk owner accepts or escalates through an informed, documented decision
Believing insurance or outsourcing transfers all risk Transfer shifts financial loss; accountability, regulatory and reputational risk stay
Every privacy activity requires consent Determine the applicable lawful basis and any additional conditions
Encryption establishes privacy compliance Also assess purpose, minimization, transparency, rights and retention
A sensitive-data label protects the file The label guides handling; access and other safeguards implement the rules
One directory disablement completes a leaver process Cover applications, cloud sessions, privileged access, physical access and shared secrets
A right-to-audit clause shows vendor controls work Assurance comes from exercising it or from a relevant SOC 2 Type II report
Any SOC 2 Type II report covers the customer's needs Read scope, period, criteria, exceptions, subservices and CUECs
Using uptime statistics to show the board the value of IT Use an IT balanced scorecard linked to business objectives
Buying a tool as the first step of a new program Policy, scope and ownership come first; tools follow the policy

An integrated case

A retailer launches a loyalty service through a cloud supplier. Enrollment rises, but management cannot identify all location-data exports. The vendor's assurance report ends four months before launch, no one owns the business benefit measure, and a former contractor retains access to the export tool.

Separate the issues: data inventory and purpose, supplier assurance coverage, benefit ownership and leaver access. Establish the actual processing and access, assess the business and privacy exposure, obtain evidence for the missing period, and communicate supported findings to the responsible owners. Rising enrollment does not resolve the other risks. Internal audit recommends and evaluates; management decides and implements corrective action.

Decision owners

The board or governing body sets direction, appetite and oversight, with clear boundaries on delegation. Executive management translates direction into plans, resources and operation, and keeps its accountability when sourcing work. The business or data owner defines value, classification, access expectations and outcomes, while custodians implement. The supplier owner monitors service commitments, assurance and exit readiness, because a contract does not demonstrate actual performance.

Threats and control purpose

Risk Preventive Detective Corrective
Misaligned investment Business-case and architecture approval Benefit and portfolio performance review Reprioritize, redesign or terminate through authority
Supplier control failure Risk-based selection and contractual requirements Relevant assurance, incidents and service monitoring Remediation, tested contingency or controlled exit

Practice questions

Question 1. A retailer relies on its cloud supplier's SOC 2 Type II report for a new loyalty service. The report period ended four months before the service went live. Which action BEST addresses this gap?

A) Rely on the report, since Type II tests operating effectiveness B) Obtain evidence of control operation for the uncovered period C) Rely on the right-to-audit clause in the supplier contract D) Accept a supplier statement that no controls have changed

Answer: B. A Type II report covers only its own period. A clause is a right, not assurance, and a statement is a representation, not evidence.

Question 2. A file share holding customer payment records is labeled Confidential under the classification policy. Testing shows that every employee can open it. What is the GREATEST concern?

A) The label was applied without the data owner's review B) The classification scheme has too few sensitivity levels C) Staff have not completed data handling awareness training D) Access controls do not enforce the handling the label requires

Answer: D. A label guides handling; it does not protect the file. The exposure exists until access controls implement the rule.

Question 3. Management outsources payroll processing to a provider and buys cyber insurance for the related systems, stating that the risk is now transferred. Which statement BEST describes the enterprise's position?

A) Financial loss is shared, but accountability is retained B) Payroll compliance obligations now rest with the provider C) Regulatory exposure moves to the insurer under the policy D) Residual risk is within appetite, so monitoring can stop

Answer: A. Transfer shifts financial loss. Compliance, regulatory and reputational exposure stay with the enterprise, and the supplier still needs monitoring.
