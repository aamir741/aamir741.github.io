CISA Domain 2.10: IT Performance Monitoring and Reporting

CISA Domain 2.10: KPI, KGI, KRI and KCI, performance optimization, CMMI maturity levels, Six Sigma, the IT balanced scorecard and how auditors test a reported measure.

Muhammad Aamir · 26 November 2026

A dashboard shows 100 percent of supplier reviews completed on time. Is the control working? On the CISA exam, not necessarily. This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.10: how performance monitoring turns strategy and risk expectations into information that supports decisions, and how the IS auditor tests a reported number before relying on it.

2.10 What performance monitoring does

Reports should help the reader understand results and act on deviations. A good measure has a defined:

  • purpose and calculation
  • owner and source
  • period and target
  • action threshold

An unexplained green indicator offers little assurance.

2.10.1 Key performance indicators

A key performance indicator (KPI) measures performance relevant to an objective or process. A key goal indicator (KGI) describes whether a desired outcome has been achieved. The distinction depends on the level: an operating target for one team may be an input to a wider business outcome. Don't classify every KPI as automatically leading, or every count as useful.

2.10.2 Key risk indicators

A key risk indicator (KRI) signals exposure or a change in the conditions associated with risk. It should connect to a risk statement and threshold, with an owner and a response. An increase in overdue critical vulnerabilities can signal rising exposure; it does not by itself quantify the probability of a future breach.

2.10.3 Key control indicators

A key control indicator (KCI) measures the operation or effectiveness of a control. The proportion of privileged accounts reviewed by their due date measures review coverage. Whether those reviews actually identify and remove inappropriate access may need additional evidence. A completed checklist is not the same as effective challenge.

Measure Question Retailer example
KPI How is the process or service performing? Orders processed within the service target
KGI Was the desired outcome achieved? Agreed reduction in failed checkouts achieved this quarter
KRI What signals increased exposure? Unresolved high-risk supplier control exceptions past deadline
KCI Is a particular control operating as intended? Required supplier reviews completed with exceptions followed up
CSF What must go well for the objective to succeed? Reliable stock information and enough peak capacity

Example: a dashboard reports 100 percent supplier review completion, but reviewers upload reports without reading exceptions or checking complementary user entity controls (CUECs). Completion is a useful activity measure, but it doesn't establish that the control achieves its purpose. Refine the measure and test review quality.

2.10.4 Performance optimization

Optimization improves performance in relation to business needs, resource constraints and risk. Understand the baseline and root cause before selecting an improvement. Critical success factors include leadership support, clear goals, reliable data, employee involvement and continuing review. A process can become faster while becoming less controlled.

  • Benchmarking compares performance with a relevant peer or reference.
  • Root cause analysis investigates why a problem recurs.
  • Business process reengineering (BPR) redesigns a process fundamentally; incremental improvement makes smaller repeated changes.

Capability concerns how well a particular process or practice performs. Maturity describes a broader progression in institutionalized, managed and improving practices under the chosen model. State which model is used; similar level names don't make different scales interchangeable.

CMMI maturity level Meaning
0 Incomplete Work may not be completed; the approach is not established
1 Initial Work occurs but is unpredictable and reactive
2 Managed Projects are planned and controlled
3 Defined Organizational standards guide the work
4 Quantitatively Managed Quantitative understanding supports predictability and control
5 Optimizing Continuous improvement and adaptation are embedded

CMMI is often taught as levels 1 to 5, but the published model also identifies level 0. Capability levels for individual practice areas use a different scale, and the highest level is not automatically the right investment for every process. O-ISM3 is The Open Group's process-based approach to security management maturity; a maturity label alone doesn't prove a control is effective.

2.10.5 Approaches and techniques

Six Sigma uses data and structured analysis to reduce defects and variation, through DMAIC: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control. Agile practices use feedback, collaboration and incremental delivery, but they don't remove the need for governance, authorization or evidence.

A balanced scorecard (BSC) links strategic objectives to measures across several perspectives:

Enterprise perspective IT perspective Illustrative measure
Financial Corporate contribution Verified benefits compared with life cycle cost
Customer User orientation Business-user satisfaction and service completion
Internal process Operational excellence Transaction reliability, incident recurrence
Learning and growth Future orientation Skills for the roadmap, fewer obsolete dependencies

Uptime alone can't establish whether an investment creates the intended business benefit. Portfolio management selects, balances and steers investments; the scorecard communicates objectives and measures progress. They complement each other. Evaluate life cycle cost and benefits together, since a lower purchase price may bring higher support or exit costs.

NIST CSF 2.0 has six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. They organize connected outcomes, not a mandatory linear sequence, and a profile is not certification that controls operate effectively.

Auditor perspective: don't trust the green

Reperform selected metric calculations, reconcile source populations, inspect exclusions and test whether adverse results led to action. Consider incentives: a closure rate can improve because tickets are closed prematurely. Compare closure speed with reopening rates and user outcomes, and review trends and distribution as well as averages.

Example: a security dashboard shows fewer overdue high-risk actions after the query starts excluding actions reassigned to suppliers. Reconcile the population before and after the change, recalculate using the agreed rule, trace significant exceptions to decisions and explain the effect on trends.

Practice questions

1. A dashboard shows 100 percent of required supplier reviews completed on time, but reviewers did not read exceptions or check CUECs. What is the IS auditor's BEST conclusion? Answer: completion alone doesn't show the control is effective. It's an activity measure; test review quality.

2. Reported service availability improved sharply after maintenance outages were excluded from the calculation. What should the IS auditor do FIRST? Answer: recalculate it using the agreed service window, then judge contractual treatment and business impact.
