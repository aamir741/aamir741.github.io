CISA Domain 2.5: Enterprise Risk Management

CISA Domain 2.5: risk appetite, tolerance and capacity, the risk life cycle, risk responses, SLE, ARO and ALE, and authorized risk acceptance.

Muhammad Aamir · 21 November 2026

A high-risk finding has sat untreated for a year, and nobody owns it. Has management accepted that risk? On the CISA exam, almost certainly not. This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.5: how enterprise risk management (ERM) identifies, assesses, treats and monitors the uncertainty that affects business objectives.

2.5 What ERM covers

IT risk belongs within the enterprise view, because one technology incident can affect financial results, customer safety, legal compliance and strategy at the same time.

  • A threat is a potential cause of harm.
  • A vulnerability is a weakness that can be exploited.
  • An impact is a consequence for the enterprise.

Risk analysis considers the likelihood and impact of a credible event in its business context. A vulnerability severity score alone does not establish business risk.

2.5.1 Developing a risk management program

Begin with the enterprise's objectives, context and risk criteria. Establish sponsorship, policy, appetite, responsibilities, assessment methods, reporting and escalation. Assign accountable owners with authority to decide or escalate, and embed the process in strategy, projects, operations, procurement and change rather than running a disconnected annual exercise.

Term Meaning
Risk appetite The amount and type of risk the enterprise is willing to pursue or retain
Risk tolerance Acceptable variation or boundaries around a particular objective or risk
Risk capacity The risk the enterprise can bear without threatening its viability
Inherent risk Exposure before the relevant controls or treatment
Residual risk Exposure remaining after controls, compared with approved criteria

Appetite is a deliberate choice; capacity is the ability to absorb exposure. Neither permits management to disregard a legal obligation. The board oversees appetite, management implements the program, risk owners decide within delegated limits, and internal audit evaluates the arrangements without accepting risk on management's behalf.

Example: a retailer accepts some fluctuation in online demand but sets a specific limit for checkout disruption. A cost-saving proposal that may exceed that limit needs the established escalation process. An IT manager cannot silently redefine tolerance to make it look acceptable.

2.5.2 Risk management life cycle

The cycle is iterative, with communication and consultation throughout.

  1. Identification. Consider assets, dependencies, processes, people, suppliers, projects and emerging change. Describe the cause, event and consequence clearly. A risk register records the risk, objective, owner, assessment, controls, response, action dates, residual exposure and review status. A long register with no accountable owners is not an effective program.
  2. Assessment. Analyze likelihood and impact over a stated period with consistent definitions, then evaluate against appetite and tolerance. Several small risks may combine into a significant concentration risk. A likelihood-times-impact score supports prioritization but does not replace evidence or an authorized decision.
  3. Response. Choose a response, then define actions, owners, deadlines and expected residual risk.
  4. Monitoring and reporting. Check that responses are implemented and controls work, using incident trends and risk and control indicators. Escalate to someone with authority, and reassess after a new supplier, acquisition, major system change or significant incident.
Response What changes
Avoid Stop or do not start the activity
Mitigate / reduce Reduce likelihood, impact or both through controls
Share / transfer Allocate defined consequences through insurance or contract terms
Accept Retain exposure through an authorized, informed decision

Cost is one consideration, not the only one. A control may be required by law even where expected-loss math looks unfavorable.

Audit focus: sample register entries and trace them to assessments, owners, implemented actions and monitoring. Challenge unjustified ratings, overdue actions and acceptance outside delegated authority, and explain the business exposure, not only the missing document.

2.5.3 Risk analysis methods

  • Qualitative: defined ratings such as low, medium and high.
  • Semiquantitative: scores or bands; multiplying arbitrary scores can suggest false precision.
  • Quantitative: numerical frequencies and losses, often in money; uncertain inputs and rare events affect the result.
Measure Formula
SLE Asset value (AV) × exposure factor (EF)
ARO Expected events per year (may be fractional)
ALE SLE × ARO
Annual net benefit ALE before − ALE after − annual control cost

Worked example: AV $100,000 × EF 0.25 = SLE $25,000. With ARO 2, ALE = $50,000. A control reduces ALE to $15,000 and costs $20,000 a year, so the net benefit is $50,000 − $15,000 − $20,000 = $15,000. The remaining $15,000 is still exposure.

An ARO of 0.2 means one event in five years under the model, not which year. Don't multiply EF twice, and treat "inherent risk minus control" as conceptual only.

Auditor perspective: acceptance versus inaction

Leaving a risk untreated is not authorized acceptance. Determine the exposure, obligations and delegated authority, and obtain an informed decision by the proper owner. If the modeled loss is within an owner's financial delegation but the outage exposure exceeds the limit for a critical service, compare each dimension and escalate. Audit assesses and recommends; it never becomes the risk owner.

Practice questions

1. An IS auditor finds a high-rated risk that has stayed untreated for a year with no assigned owner. What should the auditor do FIRST? Answer: determine who has authority to decide on it. Inaction is not acceptance, and audit cannot accept the risk itself.

2. An asset is valued at $200,000 with an exposure factor of 0.5 and an ARO of 0.1. What is the ALE? Answer: $10,000 per year. SLE is $100,000, multiplied by 0.1.
