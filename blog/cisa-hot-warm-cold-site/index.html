Hot vs Warm vs Cold Site: CISA Domain 4 Explained Simply

The difference between hot, warm and cold recovery sites for the CISA exam, how RTO decides the choice, a practice question and the classic exam trap.

Muhammad Aamir · 17 October 2026

Recovery sites appear again and again in CISA preparation, under Domain 4: Information Systems Operations and Business Resilience. The concept is simple once you see it as a trade-off between speed and cost.

The three main types of recovery site

Site What is in place Typical recovery
Hot site Fully configured: hardware, software, network and data ready to run Within hours, or even minutes
Warm site Partially configured: network connections and some equipment, but not the main computer Days
Cold site Only the basic environment: power, cooling, space and flooring. No computer equipment Weeks

Two more you may meet: a mobile site (a specially designed trailer that can be moved to where it is needed) and a reciprocal agreement (two organisations agree to use each other's facilities in a disaster).

Faster recovery means higher cost

A hot site is the fastest and the most expensive, because you are paying for a second, ready-to-run environment. A cold site is the cheapest and the slowest. The recovery time objective (RTO), the maximum acceptable downtime agreed by the business, decides which one is appropriate:

  • RTO of a few hours → hot site
  • RTO of a few days → warm site may be acceptable
  • RTO of weeks → a cold site may be enough

Practice question

A bank's payment system must be running again within a few hours of a disaster. Which recovery option BEST meets this requirement?

  • A. Cold site with an equipment delivery contract
  • B. Warm site with network connections in place
  • C. Hot site with fully configured systems
  • D. Reciprocal agreement with a partner bank

Answer: C. Only a hot site is ready to operate within hours.

Why not the others?

  • A, cold site: a delivery contract helps, however equipment still has to arrive, be installed and configured, which takes days or weeks.
  • B, warm site: network connectivity is valuable, however the main computer is missing, so recovery cannot happen within hours.
  • D, reciprocal agreement: it is cheap, however capacity is not guaranteed when you need it and such agreements are hard to test and enforce.

The exam trap

Many candidates assume a warm site already has the main computer. It does not. A warm site has network connections and selected peripheral equipment; only a hot site is ready to run.

Keep the qualifier in mind as well: when a question asks for the BEST option, more than one answer may work eventually. Pick the one that meets the stated requirement, here, recovery within hours.
