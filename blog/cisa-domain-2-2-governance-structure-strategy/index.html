CISA Domain 2.2: Organizational Structure, IT Governance and IT Strategy

CISA Domain 2.2: governance vs management, COBIT, the Three Lines Model, IS strategy, committees, data ownership and separation of duties.

Muhammad Aamir · 18 November 2026

The board approves a digital strategy, but a year later the money has gone to projects with no link to it. Is the strategy document enough evidence of alignment? This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.2: who decides, whose needs the decisions serve, and how results are overseen.

2.2.1 Enterprise governance of information and technology

EGIT covers information and technology across the whole business, not just the IT department. The governing body evaluates stakeholder needs, directs priorities and monitors results, with a focus on value and exposure. Management plans and delivers within that direction. Both monitor, but for different purposes.

Governance sets direction. Management delivers and reports. Audit supplies independent assurance.

2.2.2 Good practices for EGIT

Good governance combines strategic alignment, value delivery, risk management, resource management and performance measurement. A low-cost service that blocks a critical business transaction is not a success just because it met its budget.

In COBIT, governance is EDM (Evaluate, Direct, Monitor). Management domains are APO (Align, Plan and Organize), BAI (Build, Acquire and Implement), DSS (Deliver, Service and Support) and MEA (Monitor, Evaluate and Assess).

Reference Primary purpose
ISO/IEC 38500 Governance principles for governing bodies
ITIL / ISO/IEC 20000 Service management practices / requirements
ISO/IEC 27001 Information security management system requirements
NIST CSF 2.0 Six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
CMMI Capability and process improvement, not a security certification

Framework adoption is evidence of an approach, not proof that controls operate effectively.

2.2.3 Audit's role and the Three Lines Model

The IS auditor evaluates whether governance supports business objectives, assigns accountability, manages significant risk and enables useful oversight.

  • First line: process owners deliver services and own the risk.
  • Second line: risk, security oversight and compliance teams advise, monitor and challenge. They remain management roles.
  • Third line: internal audit gives independent assurance to the governing body, and must not take management responsibility for what it later audits.

2.2.4 Information security governance

Security governance sets priorities, accountability, resources, policy direction and oversight. A firewall configuration is an operating control; deciding security direction and holding management accountable are governance.

2.2.5 and 2.2.6 IS strategy and strategic planning

The IS strategy explains how systems and technology enable business objectives, and business and IT build it together. Alignment shows when initiatives map to outcomes, with sponsors, benefits, resources and measures. A list of products to buy is not a strategy.

Planning assesses the current state, defines target capabilities, identifies gaps and prioritizes a roadmap. Review strategy when conditions change; a fixed five-year cycle does not justify an obsolete plan. Auditors trace major initiatives to strategy, approved cases, benefit owners and actual spending.

2.2.7 Business intelligence

A dashboard's appearance does not prove its data are complete. Metadata describes meaning, format, owner and rules. Lineage traces a figure back to authorized source transactions. When sales and finance define "active customer" differently, business owners agree the definition first.

2.2.8 Organizational structure

Body or role Primary responsibility
IT strategy committee Advises the board on IT direction and value
IT steering committee Prioritizes initiatives, allocates resources, monitors delivery
Data owner Decides classification and authorized access
Data custodian Operates technical safeguards and storage
Security administrator Implements approved privileges

Separation of duties splits authorization, execution, recording and reconciliation so one person cannot create an error and conceal it. In a RACI matrix, each activity needs one clear Accountable owner. Where a small team cannot separate roles, a compensating control must address the specific conflict, such as independent review of protected change logs against approved requests.

2.2.9 Auditing IT governance

Corroborate charters, strategy and reports with interviews and samples of real decisions. Red flags include repeated unapproved purchases, unresolved ownership, obsolete plans and projects monitored only for spending. Gap analysis compares the current state with an agreed target; management selects priorities and accepts residual risk, while audit evaluates the evidence.

Practice questions

1. An IS auditor is assessing whether IT investments support the business strategy. Which evidence would BEST demonstrate alignment? A. A board-approved IT strategy document B. Initiatives traced to objectives, sponsors and realized benefits C. Formal adoption of the COBIT framework D. Steering committee minutes showing IT attendance

Answer: B. A strategy or framework shows an approach, not that funding follows it.

2. Which party should decide whether a business user may access customer account data? A. The security administrator B. The data custodian C. The business data owner D. The IT steering committee

Answer: C. Administrators and custodians implement access under the owner's direction.
