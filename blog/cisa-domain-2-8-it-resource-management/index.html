CISA Domain 2.8: IT Resource Management

CISA Domain 2.8: the value of IT, portfolio management, HR controls and key person risk, enterprise change management, chargeback, TCO and business case measures.

Muhammad Aamir · 24 November 2026

Only one engineer can recover a business-critical database. There's a signed recovery plan and an unused training budget. Is the key-person risk resolved? On the CISA exam, no. This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.8: how IT resource management matches people, technology, information, finances and service capacity to business priorities, while maintaining appropriate control.

2.8.1 Value of IT

IT value is the contribution technology makes to business outcomes, considered alongside cost and risk. Benefits can include revenue growth, faster service, fewer errors, improved resilience or necessary compliance.

A system delivered on time is an output; improved order fulfilment is an outcome. Assign a business benefit owner and measure whether the outcome occurs.

2.8.2 Implementing IT portfolio management

A portfolio groups investments, programs, projects or services so they can be selected and managed together. Compare strategic contribution, expected benefits, costs, risk, dependencies and resource constraints. Portfolio management can defer, stop or redirect an investment when assumptions change; it is more than maintaining a project list.

Consider the full life cycle, including ongoing service and retirement. A technically successful project may deliver little value if users do not adopt it. Business owners remain responsible for realizing benefits.

Example: three divisions request new analytics products while a common data-quality issue prevents reliable reporting. Portfolio review may prioritize the shared data capability before buying three tools, documenting expected benefit, dependencies, resource limits and the exposure of delay.

2.8.3 IT management practices

Management turns direction into operating plans, assigned responsibilities, service agreements, budgets and review routines. Maintain asset and service inventories, capacity forecasts, skills and supplier visibility. Monitor deviations and make authorized changes; an approved annual budget is not a permanent answer to changing demand.

2.8.4 Human resource management

People controls address competence, integrity, availability and accountability throughout employment, for employees and relevant contractors. HR and line management define the employment process; IT and security implement the associated access, training and technical safeguards.

Practice What the auditor looks for
Recruiting and hiring Defined duties, verified qualifications, proportionate lawful screening
Handbook, terms and training Communicated conduct, confidentiality, IP, evidence of completion
Performance and discipline Agreed goals and fair review; consistent, documented handling
Promotion and transfers Reassessed access; previous privileges removed
Required vacations A real, uninterrupted absence with work covered by someone else
Termination Timely access removal, asset return, preserved records

Key person risk: cross-training builds backup capability, job rotation moves people between duties, and succession planning prepares replacements. Granting every backup administrator permanent unrestricted access may reduce staffing dependence while creating a control conflict.

Mandatory vacation and rotation can expose irregularities that depend on continuous concealment, but they do not guarantee fraud detection, particularly with collusion or weak reviews.

Leavers and movers: use an authoritative HR event to trigger a coordinated process covering network, cloud, application, remote, physical and privileged access, plus devices, tokens and shared secrets. Removing a directory account alone may leave independent SaaS sessions or vendor access active.

Audit focus: compare selected joiners, movers and leavers with actual privileges and completion times, across both HR and contractor records.

2.8.5 Enterprise change management

Enterprise change management prepares the organization to adopt changes in processes, technology, structure or behavior: sponsorship, communication, training, readiness, resistance and sustained adoption. Technical change control governs authorized alterations to systems. The two are complementary.

Training attendance is only one measure. Example: a new procurement system is technically stable, but employees keep ordering outside it because approval roles and training are unclear. More server capacity does not fix an adoption failure.

2.8.6 Financial management practices

Budgets should reflect business priorities, recurring obligations, projects and future capacity. Investigate variances, and remember that low spending is not automatically favorable if critical maintenance or controls were deferred.

Concept Meaning
Chargeback Charge consuming business units for allocated IT costs
Showback Report consumption and allocated cost without charging it
CapEx / OpEx Different accounting treatment under the applicable policy
Capitalized software Eligible costs only, not all development spending
TCO Acquisition, operation, support, security, change and exit costs
Life cycle cost Costs across every stage, including transition and retirement

A business case may use ROI, NPV, IRR (the rate at which NPV is zero) and payback. They answer different questions; simple payback omits later benefits and the time value of money.

2.8.7 Information security management

Information security management implements the security direction through policies, risk assessment, controls, awareness, incident arrangements and monitoring. Security technology is part of the management system, not its entirety. Security managers report exposure and control performance; business and risk owners make authorized decisions.

Auditor perspective: people, capacity, cost and knowledge

Concentrated expertise, unsupported technology and unplanned license costs can threaten delivery even when utilization looks low. For the single-engineer database, verify trained alternate coverage, current instructions and an observed recovery exercise. Knowledge transfer is demonstrated when another authorized person completes the task.

Cost comparisons should include acquisition, operation, support, integration, migration, security and exit costs. A low initial cloud price can be outweighed by transfer charges or retained staffing. If shared cloud costs are allocated by account count while one batch team uses most of the compute, inspect the allocation basis, consumption data, approvals and dispute handling.

Practice questions

1. Only one engineer can recover a business-critical database, and a signed recovery plan exists. Which action BEST addresses the key-person risk? Answer: verify an alternate can perform an observed recovery. Budget and signatures do not demonstrate capability, and permanent access for everyone creates a control conflict.

2. An IS auditor finds that leavers' directory accounts are disabled promptly after HR notification. What is the auditor's BEST next step? Answer: test leavers' SaaS, remote and privileged access. One access path is not every access path.
