How to Spot a Phishing Scam in 10 Seconds

The four red flags of a phishing message, the golden rule about PINs, passwords and OTPs, and what to do if in doubt. From ICAP CAF-3 Data, Systems & Risks.

Muhammad Aamir · 19 October 2026

Your bank says your account will be blocked today unless you click a link. Real or fake? Most scam messages can be spotted in seconds once you know what to look for.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a fake message, by SMS, email, WhatsApp or phone call, designed to steal your passwords or money.

  • Ordinary phishing is a wide net: the same message sent to thousands of people, hoping a few will bite.
  • Spear phishing is a harpoon: a researched message aimed at you by name, often pretending to come from your boss, your bank or a government office.

The 4 red flags

Red flag What it looks like
⏰ Urgency "Act now or your account will be blocked"
🔗 Strange link A short or unfamiliar web address
✉️ Look-alike sender One letter changed, such as paypa1.com instead of paypal.com
🔑 Asks for secrets Your PIN, password or one-time code (OTP)

The golden rule

No genuine bank will ever ask for your PIN, password or OTP by call, SMS or email.

If in doubt: stop, verify, report

  1. Stop. Don't click the link or reply.
  2. Verify. Call the official number on your card or the bank's website, never a number given in the message.
  3. Report. Report and block the sender, and warn family members, especially parents.

Quick test

Which message is MOST likely a phishing attempt?

  • A. "Your monthly statement is ready in the bank app"
  • B. "URGENT: account blocked in 2 hours. Verify here: bit.ly/x7k"
  • C. "Your OTP for the payment you just made is 4821"
  • D. "Reminder: your card bill is due on the 25th"

Answer: B. It combines urgency, a short link and a threat.

For organisations

Phishing mainly attacks confidentiality: the attacker wants credentials and data. The strongest defences are regular awareness training, two-factor authentication and an easy way for staff to report suspicious emails.
