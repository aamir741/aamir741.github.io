CISA Domain 1.7: Audit Evidence Collection Techniques

CISA Domain 1.7: sufficient and appropriate evidence, eight collection techniques, matching evidence to the claim, and chain of custody.

Muhammad Aamir · 12 November 2026

Every backup job shows success. Does that prove the data can be recovered? This lesson covers CISA Domain 1, Section 1.7: how an IS auditor collects evidence that supports a finding or conclusion against criteria, and keeps that evidence trustworthy.

Sufficient and appropriate evidence

Match evidence to the claim, scope and period. A successful backup job addresses job completion. Recoverability needs evidence that the required data can be restored and used, and a recovery time target needs reliable timing.

Property Question
Sufficiency Is there enough evidence for the scope and conclusion?
Relevance Does it address the claim?
Reliability Can the source, collection and accuracy be trusted?

Relevance and reliability together make up appropriateness. Consider risk, coverage and quality together. A folder of screenshots from the wrong environment contributes little to a conclusion about production.

Judging reliability

Look at the provider (independence, competence, objectivity) and the evidence (authenticity, completeness, accuracy, timing). Directly obtained evidence and independent corroboration can be persuasive when they match the objective and the method is sound.

Source What to check
Auditor-obtained Procedure suitability and input completeness
Independent external Respondent knowledge and independence, authenticity, scope and date
Internal records and reports Creation, processing, access and retention controls, including filters
Oral evidence Records, observation or tests that corroborate significant claims
Copies and extracts Traceability, completeness and integrity

Evidence collection techniques

Technique Auditor action
Inquiry Ask a knowledgeable person, then corroborate important statements
Inspection Examine records, configurations, contracts or assets
Observation Watch an activity being performed
Walkthrough Follow actual activity through the process to identify control points
External confirmation Obtain a response directly from a knowledgeable external party
Recalculation Independently check mathematical accuracy
Reperformance Independently repeat a procedure or control
Analytical procedures Examine relationships, trends and unusual differences

Exam trap: watching a reconciliation is observation, checking its arithmetic is recalculation, and repeating the underlying check is reperformance. Control the delivery and receipt of external confirmations, and consider alternatives if the response is incomplete or unavailable.

Reviewing IS documents and records

Check that documents reflect reality: organization charts against actual responsibilities and rights, policies against the version applicable in the audit period and actual practice, standards against applicable systems and authorized deviations, and IS documentation against scope, origin, approval history and the actual version.

Matching evidence to the claim

A critical application requires daily backups, monthly recovery tests and recovery within four hours during each test.

Claim Evidence
Required backups completed Reconcile the expected schedule with execution records
A copy could be restored and used Observe an authorized restore or inspect reliable, validated test records
Recovery met the target Verify agreed start and finish events, recalculate elapsed time
Monthly tests took place Compare the expected schedule with dated records

A test taking four hours and twenty minutes demonstrates recovery under those conditions and shows the target was exceeded. A conclusion about monthly testing needs evidence covering the relevant months.

Preserving evidence and chain of custody

Record the source system, extraction method and filters, audit period, collection date and time zone, record totals and collector. Chain of custody is the documented history of who collected, held, transferred and examined evidence. Use it when identity or integrity may be challenged.

  1. Identify and collect
  2. Preserve and protect (analyze a controlled working copy)
  3. Record transfers
  4. Retain and reference in work papers

A matching cryptographic hash helps show content is unchanged since collection. Whether the original source was accurate is a separate question.

1.7.1 Interviewing and observing personnel

Interview people who perform the work and those who supervise it. Ask open questions and request a demonstration or supporting record. If a manager says all monthly tests occurred but a month is missing from the register, check the register's completeness, other records and the system covered.

Observation shows who performs a task and how exceptions are handled, but people can change their behavior while observed. Use records for the relevant period.

Practice questions

1. An IS auditor must conclude whether data for a critical application can be recovered. Which evidence is MOST relevant? A. Backup job reports showing successful completion B. A signed statement from the backup administrator C. The approved backup policy and backup schedule D. A validated restore of the application and data

Answer: D. Job reports show job completion, not recoverability.

2. An IS auditor wants to verify that the elapsed time recorded for a recovery test is accurate. Which technique is BEST? A. Recalculating it from the agreed start and finish events B. Observing the next scheduled recovery test as it runs C. Asking the recovery team lead to explain the timing D. Inspecting the recovery plan for the approved target

Answer: A. Accuracy of a calculation calls for recalculation.
