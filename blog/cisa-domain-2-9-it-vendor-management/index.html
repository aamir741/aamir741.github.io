CISA Domain 2.9: IT Vendor Management

CISA Domain 2.9: sourcing models, due diligence, SLAs and right to audit, SOC 1, SOC 2 and SOC 3 reports, escrow, cloud governance, capacity planning and supplier exit.

Muhammad Aamir · 25 November 2026

Your payroll provider's SOC report covers January to June, but you need assurance for the whole year, and the provider sends a bridge letter. Is that enough? On the CISA exam, no. This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.9: how vendor management governs a supplier relationship from selection through delivery, change and exit. Accountability for the enterprise's objectives and obligations always remains with the enterprise.

2.9.1 Sourcing practices

Sourcing choice Potential benefit Exposure to assess
Insourcing Direct control, retained knowledge Skills, capacity, key-person dependence
Outsourcing Expertise, scale, delivery capability Dependency, visibility, contract or transition failure
Selective sourcing Best arrangement per service Hand-offs and unclear accountability between suppliers
Onshore / nearshore / offshore Different locations, skills, hours Jurisdiction, language, time zone, data flows
Cloud services Rapid shared infrastructure, platforms, applications Shared responsibility, concentration, cost, exit

Onshore, nearshore and offshore describe geography; onsite and offsite describe whether work happens at the customer's premises. Neither describes the strength of the supplier's controls. A low bid can conceal transition costs, retained staffing, integration work or lock-in, and the best model depends on business need and risk.

2.9.2 Outsourcing practices and strategies

Due diligence is a proportionate investigation of a proposed supplier before deciding to rely on it. Classify the service's criticality and data, assess alternatives and retained responsibilities, and review capability, control environment, security, privacy, financial viability, resilience, subcontractors, location and references. A completed questionnaire is useful only when its answers are assessed and important claims corroborated. A vendor's size or reputation does not replace this assessment.

The contract defines enforceable duties and remedies. An SLA specifies measurable service commitments; an OLA coordinates supporting internal teams. Align them so end-to-end commitments can actually be delivered. The contract should cover scope and responsibilities, service levels, security and privacy, incidents and continuity, assurance and access, subcontracting and change, commercial and legal terms, and termination and exit.

A right-to-audit clause is useful only if it can be exercised and provides adequate coverage. Independent reports support oversight but do not automatically replace audit or regulatory access rights. An NDA complements access and data-handling controls rather than replacing them. A certificate concerns a defined scope, so verify the purchased service and locations are covered.

Third-party audit reports

Report Addresses
SOC 1 Controls relevant to customers' internal control over financial reporting
SOC 2 Controls against the applicable Trust Services Criteria
SOC 3 Assurance communicated more generally, with less detail
Type 1 Description and suitability of control design at a specified date
Type 2 Design plus operating effectiveness over a specified period

SOC 2 is not simply a higher grade of SOC 1; the subject and audience differ. Read the scope, criteria, opinion, tests, exceptions and dates, and consider subservice organizations, report-period gaps and complementary user entity controls (CUECs). A bridge letter is a management statement, not an independent extension of the examination.

Software escrow places agreed materials, usually source code, with an independent holder for release under defined conditions. It is of limited value if no one can build, maintain or operate the released software.

Service level management is the continuing process of agreeing, measuring, reviewing and improving commitments. Signing an SLA does not establish it, and a supplier can meet an infrastructure uptime target while the business transaction still fails.

2.9.3 Cloud governance

Model Provider typically manages Customer still manages
IaaS Facilities, hardware, virtualization Guest OS, applications, data, identities, configuration
PaaS Infrastructure and the platform or runtime Application code, data, identities
SaaS The application and service stack Authorized use, data, users, configuration, integration

The actual service and contract set the boundary. The provider's security does not automatically secure customer permissions, sharing settings or integrations. Shadow IT is technology used outside established oversight: discover its purpose and exposure, then apply proportionate approval and control.

2.9.4 and 2.9.5 Governance in outsourcing, capacity and growth planning

Assign an internal service owner and relationship manager, set a governance calendar, and look at value, risk, cost, satisfaction and supplier viability rather than only SLA credits. Retain staff who can challenge the supplier and manage a transition.

Capacity planning covers compute, storage, network, licenses, people, funding and supplier limits. Average utilization can hide a bottleneck: a retailer's checkout can fail during a short annual sale even though average usage is low. Cloud scalability does not remove quota, cost or architectural constraints.

2.9.6 Third-party service delivery management

Monitor reports, incidents, changes and outcomes, and reconcile supplier reports to independent logs where appropriate. Hold scheduled reviews and watch financial viability and concentration. A relationship established with good due diligence can still become unacceptable after a material change. Distinguish response time from resolution time, and availability from successful business transactions.

Audit focus: trace a critical service from due diligence through contract, transition, periodic review and exit planning, and check retained customer controls, not only the supplier's report.

Auditor perspective: assurance has boundaries

A third-party report helps only if its scope, period, criteria, exceptions and customer responsibilities fit the service. Service credits compensate defined failures but do not restore a critical service. An SBOM identifies components but does not certify a product secure. If two suppliers rely on the same cloud region and identity provider, count the shared failure domain.

Test the exit route during the contract term. A successful download demonstrates delivery of a file; validating an authorized test export shows whether the business can actually use the data, including identifiers and relationships.

Practice questions

1. A payroll provider's SOC 1 Type 2 report covers January to June, but the audit needs full-year assurance. The provider offers a bridge letter. What should the IS auditor do FIRST? Answer: obtain additional evidence for July to December. A bridge letter is a management statement, not an independent examination.

2. A company moves its HR application to a SaaS provider with a strong security certification. Which of the following remains the company's PRIMARY responsibility? Answer: managing user access and sharing configuration. The provider's security does not secure the customer's users and settings.
