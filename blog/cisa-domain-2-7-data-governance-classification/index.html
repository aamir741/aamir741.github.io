CISA Domain 2.7: Data Governance and Classification

CISA Domain 2.7: data inventory and classification, owners, stewards and custodians, data quality and lineage, lawful basis, consent, data subject rights and transborder data flows.

Muhammad Aamir · 23 November 2026

After an interface conversion, the record counts match perfectly. But an empty risk field is now being treated as low risk. Has the data been verified? On the CISA exam, no. This lesson covers CISA Domain 2, Section 2.7: how data governance assigns decision rights for data, and how classification drives handling.

2.7 What data governance is

Data governance establishes decision rights and accountability for data quality, meaning, use and protection. Classification turns differences in value and sensitivity into handling requirements. Both are business responsibilities supported by IT, security, privacy and data specialists.

2.7.1 Data inventory and classification

An information asset can be a customer dataset or business record; supporting IT assets include the applications, devices and services used to process it. Define the inventory scope so information ownership is not confused with technical custody.

A data inventory identifies information assets, systems, owners, purposes and flows. Classification assigns categories based on business value, confidentiality, integrity, availability and legal or contractual requirements. The scheme should be simple enough to use and detailed enough to distinguish materially different handling needs.

The owner determines or approves classification and access expectations; custodians implement the necessary safeguards. A label helps users apply handling rules, but a label alone does not enforce access, prevent copying or apply retention. Review classification when the information or its use changes.

Illustrative class Meaning Typical handling
Public Approved for unrestricted disclosure Maintain integrity; publish through approved channels
Internal Intended for authorized internal use Limit routine external sharing
Confidential Unauthorized disclosure could materially harm the enterprise or people Tighter access, approved sharing, appropriate protection
Restricted A particularly sensitive category under the enterprise scheme Strongest access, monitoring and handling controls

These labels are examples, not universal legal categories. Personal data are not automatically a single sensitivity class: a public business contact and a health record have different exposure. An operational dataset may need strong integrity and availability even when its confidentiality requirement is low.

Data quality and traceability

Data quality means information is fit for its intended use. Metadata explains the information; lineage traces its origin and transformations. Define shared terms, identify authoritative sources, maintain metadata and lineage, and assign stewards to resolve quality issues.

Example: finance and operations maintain different supplier bank details. A data owner defines the authoritative record and change approval; IT implements synchronization and access control; a steward monitors inconsistencies. Without these decisions, a new dashboard simply distributes conflicting data faster.

2.7.2 Legal purpose, consent and legitimate interest

A defined business purpose and an applicable lawful basis are related but different. "Useful for marketing" describes an intended purpose; it does not by itself establish legal permission. Consent is one possible basis, not a universal requirement or a cure for every processing problem.

Concept Important distinction
Legal purpose A legitimate business objective alone does not establish every legal permission needed
Consent Informed, specific and freely made; record it and support withdrawal
Legitimate interest Document the interest, necessity and balance with the person's rights
Other lawful bases Contract, legal obligation, vital interests or public task, depending on the framework

Special categories of information or particular jurisdictions may impose additional conditions.

2.7.3 Data subject rights

Frameworks may give individuals rights to information, access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, objection and protections concerning certain automated decisions. Their availability, limits and response periods depend on the applicable law. Deletion is not an unconditional instruction to destroy records that must legally be retained.

A rights process needs intake channels, proportionate identity verification, an owner, deadline tracking, system and supplier coverage, and documented decisions. Identity checking prevents disclosure to an impostor, but excessive verification can itself collect unnecessary information.

Transborder data flow

A cross-border flow can occur through hosting, replication, support access, analytics or subcontracting. Map the actual locations, parties and access arrangements, then determine the transfer requirements and safeguards. A supplier's head office does not establish where data are processed, and a security certification does not automatically resolve transfer legality, residency restrictions or conflicting laws.

Audit focus: trace a sample rights request across applications, archived records and suppliers, and check the legal basis for any refusal or exception. For transfers, corroborate contractual statements with architecture, subprocessor lists and operational access information. In the NIST Privacy Framework 1.0, Control-P supports granular processing decisions and Communicate-P supports understanding and dialogue.

Auditor perspective: counts versus meaning

Owners make business decisions; stewards coordinate definitions and quality; custodians implement handling. One person can hold several roles, but accountability and conflicts must remain clear. A confidentiality label alone may miss integrity and availability needs, so consider aggregated information and the complete data flow.

A dashboard can be accurately calculated from the wrong source or from a field whose meaning changed. A customer-risk report that treats an empty risk field as low risk after an interface conversion will pass a record-count check. Inspect mapping rules, null handling, exception queues and owner approval, trace important measures back through interfaces and transformations to authorized source records, and have the data owner resolve the definition. A record-count reconciliation checks quantity; the lineage test addresses meaning and accuracy.

Discovery and monitoring can identify unclassified sensitive content; an enterprise need not wait for a manual label on every file before detecting exposure.

Practice questions

1. After an interface conversion, record counts reconcile, but empty risk fields now appear as low risk in reports. What should the IS auditor do FIRST? Answer: trace the field through the mapping rules to the source. Counts check quantity, not meaning.

2. Who is PRIMARILY responsible for determining the classification of a customer dataset held in a business application? Answer: the business owner of the customer data. Custodians and providers implement or host; they do not decide.
