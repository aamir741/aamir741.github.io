CISA Domain 4.12: Business Impact Analysis

CISA Domain 4.12: the business impact analysis, criticality analysis and dependencies, RTO, RPO, MTD, WRT and SDO, a worked recovery timeline, shared dependencies, and setting recovery targets at the business boundary.

Muhammad Aamir · 28 November 2026

A critical application is restored within its technical target, but staff still cannot process the required volume. Was the recovery a success? Not necessarily. This lesson covers CISA Domain 4, Section 4.12: how a business impact analysis turns business needs into recovery priorities, and how the recovery objectives fit together.

4.12 What a BIA decides

A business impact analysis (BIA) determines how disruption affects business activities over time and what must be restored, when and with which resources. Business process owners provide the essential impact and dependency information; IT translates approved needs into feasible technical capabilities. The BIA informs recovery priorities and strategy.

Exam reasoning: once continuity sponsorship, policy, scope and responsibilities are established, understand business impacts and approved recovery needs before selecting a site or drafting detailed recovery procedures.

4.12.1 Criticality analysis

Identify processes and evaluate financial, operational, legal, safety, customer and reputational effects over relevant time periods. Criticality depends on consequences and timing, not system size or the loudest department. Payroll may be time-sensitive near a payment deadline; a customer transaction service may be critical throughout its operating window.

Map people, facilities, applications, data, suppliers, networks, identity, keys and infrastructure, and sequence recovery by business needs and technical dependencies. An identity service may need recovery before several higher-profile applications can be used.

Recovery objectives

Measure Meaning
RTO Target time to restore the required service capability; must fit the business interruption tolerance
RPO Target point to which data must be restored, commonly allowable data loss in time
MTO / MTD / MAO Business interruption limit before consequences become unacceptable; equivalent only if definitions match
WRT Time to reconcile backlogs and resume business work after technical restoration
SDO Required capability during alternate or recovery mode; may be reduced but must be explicit
MTBF / MTTR Average failure interval and repair time; not replacements for recovery objectives

Worked example: order processing must resume within two hours, tolerate at most 15 minutes of data loss and support 60 percent of normal volume. RTO is two hours, RPO is 15 minutes and the alternate SDO is 60 percent. If technical recovery takes 90 minutes but reconciliation needs another hour, the capability is not restored within two hours.

An RTO greater than the business tolerance is a clear mismatch, but an RTO merely equal to it may leave no room for reconciliation or activation delays.

How the objectives fit together

Where RTO means technical restoration, plan RTO plus WRT within MTD, with margin. If RTO is defined at the business-service level, work recovery is already inside it; do not add it twice. Always establish scope and start and end points.

Timeline: disruption at 10:00. MTD is four hours, so tolerance ends at 14:00. Technical RTO is two hours (12:00) and WRT is one hour (13:00), leaving one hour of margin. RPO is 15 minutes, so the recovery point must be no earlier than 09:45. A 09:50 recovery point meets it; 09:30 does not, even if the servers start by noon. SDO is a capacity target, not another interval to add to RTO.

Auditor perspective: targets at the business boundary

A shared resource must support the earliest recovery need of its dependent services unless there is a tested alternative. If applications need recovery in one, four and eight hours and share a database, a four-hour database strategy cannot satisfy the one-hour service. Averaging RTOs would miss the critical service.

If a service must be usable within two hours and needs 30 minutes of validation after its shared database is ready, the database must be ready within 90 minutes. Confirm when the service counts as usable, the acceptable age of restored data and the capacity required, trace dependencies to realistic targets and test conditions, and revisit the BIA after significant changes.

Practice questions

1. Continuity policy, sponsorship and scope are approved. What should be done NEXT? Answer: understand business impacts and approved recovery needs through a BIA.

2. Applications needing recovery in one, four and eight hours share a database with a four-hour strategy. What is the MAIN concern? Answer: the one-hour application cannot be met by the database strategy.

3. A disruption occurs at 10:00 and the RPO is 15 minutes. Which recovery point satisfies it? Answer: a 09:50 recovery point. RPO is measured backward from the disruption.
