CISA Domain 5.14: Security Incident Response Management

CISA Domain 5.14: event, alert and incident, the six response activities, CSIRT and communications, SOAR safeguards, containing without losing evidence and reading the incident's current state.

Muhammad Aamir · 28 November 2026

A confirmed compromised server is sending customer files to an external address right now. Do you notify customers, restore from backup, or contain it? On the CISA exam, once an incident is confirmed and harm is ongoing, contain first. This lesson covers CISA Domain 5, Section 5.14.

5.14 Basics

Distinguish the underlying event, a detection alert and the incident decision, which is made under the organization's criteria and authority. Incident response limits harm and supports trustworthy recovery, so prepare authority, people, tools, suppliers, communications and evidence arrangements beforehand. Response is iterative: new facts can change containment and recovery decisions. An incident response plan (IRP) specifies authority, coordination and response arrangements, including escalation, communications and responsibilities.

5.14.1 Incident response process

Activity Purpose
Preparation Incident criteria, authority, roles, contacts, playbooks, exercises, tools
Detection and analysis What happened, scope, severity, affected information, business consequences
Containment Limit ongoing harm appropriate to the situation and authority
Eradication Remove causes and persistence, correct exposure, address compromised identities
Recovery Restore validated capability, verify integrity, monitor for recurrence
Learning and improvement Document lessons and implement accountable changes

This is a teaching model, not a rule that each action waits for the last. Safety, evidence, containment, communications and recovery can require parallel work. "Always disconnect power first" is unsafe reasoning, since powering off can destroy volatile evidence or be unsafe for safety-critical systems.

5.14.2 CSIRT, plans and communications

The CSIRT coordinates technical responders with business, management, legal, privacy and communications parties. Define who can make disruptive decisions and who speaks externally, and prioritize by business impact and credible exposure rather than alert count. Plans should cover escalation, evidence, communications, supplier cooperation, restoration and records. Required notification can run alongside containment, so use applicable obligations and specialist advice.

5.14.3 SOAR

SOAR coordinates tools and playbooks and can automate enrichment, case creation or approved containment. Protect its privileged connections, validate inputs, test playbooks and use human approval where impact warrants. An erroneous automated block can interrupt a critical service, so record decisions and permit controlled reversal.

Containing with evidence, and the auditor view

Assess urgency, authority, safety and service effects, and evidence that can be preserved. Isolation or credential restriction may fit better than powering off. Ransomware can involve theft as well as encryption, so a successful restore does not close every exposure. Validate eradication, trusted restoration, data consistency and a permitted return to service, then track lessons into accountable improvements.

If harm is ongoing and authorized isolation is appropriate, containment is urgent; if the event is already contained, assess what eradication or recovery requires. For the exam: once confirmed, contain first; if contained, move to eradication and recovery. Audit reviews preparedness and response evidence without taking operational command.

Practice questions

1. A compromised server is sending customer files externally and authorized isolation is available. What should happen FIRST? Answer: isolate the server under authority while preserving evidence.

2. Which statement about incident response activities is MOST accurate? Answer: safety, evidence, containment and communications can run in parallel.

3. A SOAR playbook automatically blocks addresses that match an alert. Which safeguard is MOST important? Answer: approval for high-impact blocks, with recorded, reversible decisions.
